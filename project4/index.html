Part 1: Fit a Neural Field to a 2D Image

In Part 1 I implemented a simple neural field that maps 2D pixel coordinates (x, y) to RGB values. The model is a fully connected MLP with sinusoidal positional encoding (PE) on the input coordinates. I trained the network with mean squared error and reported Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) over iterations.

Model architecture
Input: 2D pixel coordinate (x, y) normalized to [0, 1]
Positional Encoding (L frequency bands):
PE(x) = [x, sin(2⁰πx), cos(2⁰πx), …, sin(2ᴸ⁻¹πx), cos(2ᴸ⁻¹πx)]

MLP2D:
- Depth: 4 hidden layers
- Width: 128 channels (default)
- Activation: ReLU
- Output: 3D RGB with Sigmoid in [0, 1]

Loss: MSE between predicted RGB and ground-truth pixel color
Optimizer: Adam (lr = 1e-2)
Metric: PSNR = 10 · log₁₀(1 / MSE)

Part 1.3: Hyperparameter Sweep — Width & PE Frequency

Finally I swept over two choices of width (64, 256) and two choices of maximum positional encoding frequency (L = 4, L = 10) on the fox image. Lower L and smaller width behave like a low-pass filter and blur out fine details, while larger values allow the network to represent sharper textures.

Part 2: Neural Radiance Field from Multi-view Images

In Part 2 I extended the 2D neural field idea to a full Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) on the Lego dataset. Given calibrated multi-view images and camera poses, I learn a function F(x, d) → (σ, RGB) that maps a 3D point and viewing direction to volume density and view-dependent color. Rendering is done with differentiable volume rendering along camera rays.

2.1–2.3: Rays, Sampling, and Dataloader

2.1: Pixel → Camera → World

I implemented pixel_to_camera(K, uv, s) to lift integer pixel coordinates to camera space by undoing the pinhole projection. Then transform_points(c2w, Xc) converts 3D points from camera space to world space using homogeneous coordinates and the provided c2w matrices. The ray origin is the camera center c2w[:3, 3] and the direction is the normalized vector from the origin to the world-space point at depth 1.

2.2: Sampling Points along Rays

For each ray I sample n_samples = 64 depths uniformly between near=2.0 and far=6.0. During training I add stratified perturbations within each interval to avoid always sampling the same 3D points. At test time I turn off perturbation for deterministic rendering.

2.3: RaysData Dataloader & Visualizations

My RaysData class flattens all pixels from all training images and, when sampled, returns a batch of ray origins, directions, and target RGB values. To debug coordinate conventions I used the provided viser visualizer to render camera frustums, rays, and 3D samples.

2.4: Neural Radiance Field Network

The NeRF MLP takes a 3D sample location and a 3D viewing direction. I apply high-frequency positional encoding to the position (L_xyz = 10) and a lower-frequency encoding to the viewing direction (L_dir = 4). The network first predicts a density σ ≥ 0 and a feature vector, then conditions the final RGB on both the feature and the encoded direction.

NeRF architecture (simplified)
Input:
- 3D position x (world-space), encoded with PE (L_xyz = 10)
- 3D viewing direction d, unit-normalized and PE-encoded (L_dir = 4)

Backbone:
- 8 hidden layers, width = 256
- ReLU activations
- Skip connection: concatenate encoded x into the middle layer

Heads:
- σ(x):   Linear → ReLU  (scalar density)
- feat(x): Linear       (feature vector)
- rgb(x, d): concat(feat, dir_enc) → Linear → Sigmoid (3D color)

2.5: Volume Rendering, Training Curve, and Validation PSNR

For each ray I run the NeRF network on all sampled 3D points, obtaining per-sample densities and colors. I implement the standard discrete volume rendering equation with transmittance computed via cumulative products. The rendered RGB is compared to the ground-truth pixel color with MSE, and PSNR is logged for both training and validation.

Volume rendering equation (discrete)
Given:
- σᵢ: density at sample i
- cᵢ: color at sample i
- Δᵢ: distance between samples i and i+1

Opacity:
αᵢ = 1 - exp(-σᵢ · Δᵢ)

Transmittance:
Tᵢ = Πⱼ<i (1 - αⱼ)

Rendered color:
C = Σᵢ Tᵢ · αᵢ · cᵢ

Implementation uses torch.exp, torch.cumprod, and broadcasting.

Spherical Rendering: Novel View Video

After training, I froze the NeRF and rendered images at the 60 test camera poses provided in c2ws_test, which trace out a sphere around the Lego scene. Playing these frames as a GIF produces a smooth camera orbit that shows NeRF has learned a consistent 3D representation.

Overall, this project made the connection between coordinate-based neural networks and 3D scene reconstruction feel much more concrete. Part 1 was a warm-up that treated images as continuous functions over 2D, and Part 2 extended this idea to a full volumetric radiance field that can be rendered from any viewpoint.
